## What is a 30-60-90 day plan?

A 30-60-90 day plan (also written as a 30/60/90 plan) is a document that outlines the goals and milestones an employee aims to achieve during the first 30, 60, and 90 days in a new role. It sets clear expectations, aligns the employee’s work with the company’s goals, and empowers employees to maximize their productivity.

The plan splits the first three months into three phases, and each phase builds on the one before it.

- Days 1 to 30 (learn) : The employee gets to know the company, the team, the tools, and how the role works. Goals are mostly internal and focused on learning.

- Days 31 to 60 (contribute) : The employee starts applying what they’ve learned. They take on role-specific tasks and add value to the team.

- Days 61 to 90 (own) : The employee works with more independence. They deliver results, lead small initiatives, and set goals for the months ahead.

This plan also serves as a strategic tool for both the employee and their manager to track progress, identify potential challenges early on, and make adjustments as needed to ensure the employee’s successful integration and long-term contribution to the organization.

Although 30-60-90 day plans are most often used for new hires, they can also be extremely beneficial for employees who have been promoted to a new (managerial) role or even for teams when a new project is launched. Some candidates also present one during a job interview to show how they would approach the role.

For the organization, a 30-60-90 day plan shortens the time it takes a new hire to become productive and helps managers spot skill gaps early. For the employee, it removes uncertainty about what’s expected and gives them visible wins to work toward.

### HR’s role in the 30-60-90 day plan

Around the world and across industries, HR professionals are tasked with onboarding new hires as efficiently as possible. How well an employee is onboarded impacts how quickly they become a valuable member of the team. To achieve this, HR focuses on three activities.

- Helping employees visualize how they align their work and goals to the business and connect day-to-day tasks to the larger purpose of work

- Working with managers and employees to create a clearly defined plan based on specific and measurable goals

- Regularly checking that the plan is being followed and key metrics are being achieved.

### 30-60-90 day check-in template

A 30/60-90 day check-in template helps managers review progress at the end of each phase and adjust the plan. Ask these questions at each milestone.

#### Day 30 check-in

- What have you learned about your role, the team, and how work gets done?

- Which goals have you completed, and which need more time?

- What tools, information, or support do you still need?

- Who else should you meet?

#### Day 60 check-in

- Which tasks or projects have you contributed to, and what were the results?

- Where do you feel confident, and where do you need more coaching?

- Do any goals for days 61 to 90 need to change?

#### Day 90 check-in

- Which 90-day goals did you achieve, and how did you measure them?

- What are your priorities for the next three to six months?

- How can we improve onboarding for the next new hire?

At the 90-day mark, you can follow this check-in with a formal 90-day review that rates performance and confirms the outcome of the probation period .

## How to write a 30-60-90 day plan

Whatever the job level, building an employee’s skills takes concrete performance goals. Vague goals, objectives, and metrics make a 30-60-90 day plan hard to follow and harder to measure.

For example, “increase productivity” or “become a better strategic planner” are good ambitions. But they don’t say how to get there or how progress will be measured. The goals in a 30-60-90 day plan should be realistic, quantifiable, and focused.

Here’s how to create a 30-60-90 day plan that gives new hires clear goals from day one, starting with a checklist.

### 30-60-90 day plan checklist for new employees

The 30-60-90 day plan and onboarding pack should include these elements.

- New employee welcome

- Planning get-to-know meetings

- Outlining high-level goals

- Clarifying short- and long-term priorities

- Creating SMART goals

- 30-day goals

- 60-day goals

- 90-day goals

- Defining metrics of success

- Feedback and review checkpoints

Let’s break this down.

### 1. New employee welcome

This is HR’s opportunity to lay the foundation for setting a new employee up for success. A great welcome helps new employees understand where they fit into the larger organizational structure, what is expected of them, and which activities will help them get up and running as quickly as possible. It is not a complete list of their responsibilities but rather a summary that helps them focus on the key goals and metrics they need to achieve in their first 90 days.

The welcome should include:

- Role details

- How the role supports the broader organization’s objectives

- A summary of expectations and the support the employee should expect from their manager

- The tools, system access, and equipment the employee needs are set up before their first day.

#### HR tip

Personalize the welcome to the employee, their role, and how they will add value to the business, clients and colleagues. This will set the stage for a memorable onboarding experience.

### 2. ‘Get to know’ meetings

There is a specific list of people that every new hire should connect with to help them understand their role.

The ‘get to know’ section should include:

- Who the employee is meeting, including their name, role, and responsibilities

- The key objective of the meeting

- Why this meeting will help the employee in their new role

- Topics to discuss and suggested questions the employee can ask to get the most out of the meeting.

### 3. Outlining high-level goals

A high-level view tells new employees what’s expected of them by the end of their first 90 days. It should be outcomes-based and linked to specific goals. For example, high-level goals could include contributing 15% of all new sales after 90 days, building a sales plan, or optimizing a new client onboarding process.

The long-term goals section should include:

- A list of outcomes-based goals to achieve in 30, 60, and 90 days

- Context on how each goal relates to their position. For example, “As a sales executive in this organization, one of your responsibilities is onboarding new clients.”

- A clear understanding that the employee should be aiming for fluency in their role by the 90-day mark, and that this plan will help them achieve this goal.

#### HR tip

Align long-term priorities with the organization’s key strategic goals. Then work backward to decide where the employee should be at the end of their first 90 days to support those priorities, and which 30- and 60-day goals will get them there.

#### SMART goals

As an HR professional, you can support new hires by helping them to set SMART goals that they can achieve.

SMART goals are:

- Specific

- Measurable

- Achievable

- Relevant

- Time-bound.

For example, instead of setting a goal to increase the company’s website traffic, a digital marketer’s SMART goal could be to increase traffic by 2% in the first 30 days.

The first month is about getting to know the organization and helping the new employee find their feet. Goals here should be internally oriented towards getting to know “how to do the job.” If there are external goals, they should be small and reachable.

- Work with the new employee and their manager to define between three and five goals.

90 days

The aim of the 90 day plan is to ensure an employee is effectively onboarded and adding value to the business within three months. To achieve this objective, the 90 day goals should be closely aligned to the longer term performance expectations of the role. This is very much an execution phase that leverages the learnings of the previous 60 days. Its success depends on how well the 30 day and 60 day goals were achieved.

- Ensure the employee understands this is the last month before they are expected to fully transition into their role.
- Set goals and expectations that can be used as the basis for the first performance contracting conversation.
- Allow the employee to also state what support they need during this last month to be able to fully transition into their role.

#### HR tip

The third month is when the strategy mapped out during days 31 to 60 is put into action. By this phase, the employee must be actively contributing to projects and working with their team to achieve results. Key metrics must reflect this.

### 4. Defining metrics of success

Each phase needs metrics. The measurable part of a SMART goal often covers this, but success lies in the detail. Make the deliverables for each goal quantifiable and capture them in the 30-60-90 day plan.

The right metrics help a new employee picture their role and what success looks like. They also give the employee and their manager a clear basis to measure how well onboarding is working and where the employee needs more support, tools, or insight. Metrics are also how you track progress in regular feedback sessions.

### 5. Feedback and review checkpoints

In this section, outline when the new employee’s manager and a member of the HR team will check in with them during the first 90 days. Schedule these check-ins in advance.

HR should make sure each feedback session leaves time for these four things:

- The employee to give feedback

- The manager or leadership team to give feedback

- Reviewing metrics against goals

- Planning additional support if metrics have been missed

To support managers and new employees, use the 30-60-90 day check-in template above. It helps them document what was covered in each meeting, including a recap of goals, metrics, achievements, and key outcomes or learnings.



## FAQ

What is a 30-60-90 day action plan? A 30-60-90 day plan is an essential tool that helps HR professionals to support a successful onboarding process by helping new hires and their managers to outline the new employee’s goals and objectives for the first 90 days of their employment across three 30-day phases. 

What is the 30-60-90 day rule? The 30-60-90 day rule is a common way to structure a new employee’s first three months. It splits onboarding into three 30-day phases for learning, contributing, and owning the role. Managers set goals and hold a check-in at the end of each phase so the employee knows what’s expected and gets regular feedback.

How do I write a 30-60-90 day plan for a new job? If you’re starting a new job, you can write your own 30-60-90 day plan and share it with your manager. Use the job description and your first conversations to understand your priorities. Then set two or three goals for each phase. Focus on learning in the first month, contributing in the second, and delivering results in the third. Agree on the plan with your manager and review it at each check-in.
