## What is a job offer letter?

A job offer letter is a formal document that an employer sends to the selected candidate to extend a job offer. It confirms the job title, start date, pay, and other key terms, and asks the candidate to accept in writing before onboarding begins.

- Officially extends an offer of employment to a candidate

- Provides crucial information, including start date, compensation, work hours, job title, and other important details

- Encourages employees to confirm their acceptance of the role

- Conveys your organization’s professionalism and respect for the new hire

- Gives you the opportunity to negotiate, should the employee want to discuss vacation days or remuneration

- Provides a written record of the agreed-upon terms of the role.

It’s typically sent via email once you’ve selected your candidate, often before background checks are complete and before a formal employment contract is signed. If the candidate accepts the offer, they will sign and return it; however, it’s important to note that while offer letters and contracts are often viewed as interchangeable, they are completely different documents.

An offer letter isn’t the same as an employment contract . The letter summarizes the role, pay, and benefits, while a contract sets out the detailed legal obligations of both parties.

## Free job offer letter templates

Here are five job offer letter templates in Word and PDF to help you confirm new hires quickly and clearly. Whether you’re making a formal offer, keeping things simple, making it a bit more fun, hiring an intern, making a contingent offer, or sending the offer by email, these templates cover the essentials.

### 1. Free (simple) job offer letter template

This simple template is designed for employers who want to keep things straightforward while still making a professional first impression. It’s especially handy before creating a detailed employment contract.

Sample 1: Simple job offer letter

[Company letterhead]
[Date]
[Candidate name]
[Candidate address]

Dear [Candidate name],

We’re delighted to offer you the position of [Job title] at [Company name]. You’ll report to [Manager name and title], and your start date is [Start date].

This is a [full-time/part-time] position based [on-site at location/remote/hybrid], with a standard schedule of [hours and days].

Your starting salary is [$amount] per [year/hour], paid [biweekly/monthly]. You’ll also be eligible for [benefits, such as health insurance, a retirement plan, and paid time off], as outlined in the enclosed benefits summary.

Your employment with [Company name] is at will. This means you or the company may end the employment relationship at any time, with or without cause or notice.

To accept this offer, please sign and return this letter by [Response deadline]. If you have any questions, contact [Name] at [Email or phone].

We look forward to welcoming you to the team.

Sincerely,
[Your name]
[Your title]

I accept this offer of employment as described above.
Signature ____________ Date ____________

### 2. Free internship offer letter template

Internships offer invaluable opportunities for students to acquire practical knowledge and expertise in the industries they are passionate about, and they can also add value to organizations that are interested in:

- Nurturing talent

- Bringing fresh ideas into the business

- Aligning younger team members with experienced teams

However, since internships typically have a finite duration, there are a few additional elements that need to be incorporated in the offer letter.

Sample 2: Internship offer letter sample

We’re excited to have you join us.

### 3. Free contingent offer letter template

A contingent offer letter extends the job on the condition that the candidate passes certain checks before or soon after their start date. Use it when you still need to complete background checks, reference checks, or other screening.

Sample 4: Contingent offer letter template

Dear [Candidate name],

We’re pleased to offer you the position of [Job title] at [Company name], starting on [Start date]. You’ll report to [Manager name and title].

Your starting salary is [$amount] per [year/hour], paid [frequency], and you’ll be eligible for [benefits].

This offer is contingent on the successful completion of [a background check, reference checks, verification of your educational qualifications, and a drug screening, where permitted by law]. We’ll let you know once these steps are complete. If any condition isn’t met, [Company name] may withdraw this offer.

Your employment with [Company name] is at will. This means you or the company may end the employment relationship at any time, with or without cause or notice.

Please sign and return this letter by [Response deadline] to accept the offer. Contact [Name] at [Email or phone] with any questions.

Sincerely,
[Your name]
[Your title]

## Job offer letter format and what to include

Generally, job offer letters include:

- The sender’s information

- The recipient’s information

- Salutation

- Introduction expressing enthusiasm and appreciation for the candidate’s interest in the position and their qualifications

- Position details

- The offered salary, payment frequency, and any additional compensation components

- Start date

- Reporting structure

- Employment type and work schedule

- Confidentiality and non-disclosure agreement (NDA):

- Next steps

- Contact information

- Closing, expressing enthusiasm and anticipation of the candidate’s acceptance

- Signature lines for the candidate’s signature and date

## How to write an offer letter

The offer letter template you create will be based on the role and your organization; however, there are key components all offer letters should include.

### Personalized greeting

Open by addressing the candidate by name and sharing your excitement about extending the offer. Mention the strengths and qualifications that make them a great fit for the role, and show how the offer reflects what matters to them.

### Job title and responsibilities

Include the formal job title and a brief description of the role and its responsibilities. Add the anticipated start date, full- or part-time status, work location or hybrid arrangement, and the manager the new hire will report to.

### Salary structure and employee benefits

Before you write this section, benchmark the salary against similar roles in your industry and region. Compensation is a common reason candidates turn down offers, which lowers your offer acceptance rate .

In the letter, state the salary and how often it’s paid, plus any bonus or commission structure. Then summarize the rest of the compensation package , including health and retirement benefits, paid leave, and extras such as flexible work.

#### HR tip

Be brief. Benefits will be expanded upon during the onboarding cycle. Use this section to sell the position, your company and the reasons why you are an employer of choice.

### Important legal language and contingencies

An offer letter isn’t an employment contract, but a signed letter can still create obligations. For example, listing only an annual salary can imply a guaranteed year of employment. Keep contract terms out of the letter, such as a fixed duration, detailed job duties, or grounds for termination.

Include an at-will statement to confirm that you or the employee can end the employment at any time, for any lawful reason. Most U.S. employment is at will by default, with some legal exceptions . Statements made during interviews count too, as promises of job security can be treated as part of the agreement.

Then check whether the offer needs any of the following:

- Non-disclosure, non-compete, confidentiality, or intellectual property agreements

- Immigration or visa requirements

- Contingencies, such as drug screening, criminal record checks, reference checks, education verification, or medical examinations

For offers with contingencies, use the contingent offer letter template above. Some state and local laws limit when and how you can run certain checks, so confirm timing with your legal team.

#### HR tip

Have legal review the entire letter, especially the at-will wording. You can also add a short disclaimer stating that the letter is informational and not a legally binding contract.

### Closing

In closing, you can include a deadline for when the candidate should respond. This will give the letter a sense of urgency, helping you reduce your time to hire. If the candidate does not choose to sign, a deadline will save you and them time.

You can close your letter off by expressing excitement and welcoming the candidate to the team. Include contact information so that they can reach out with questions, and a line for them to sign and date if they choose to accept.

### Tips for writing a clear job offer letter

The best letters are clear, concise, and easy to understand, while still being professional and friendly.

- Be personal

- Avoid jargon and technical terms

- Simplify any complex terms and concepts

- Ensure legal approves the letter, but do not include legal terms – this is not an employment contract

- Use active voice to keep your word count down and your writing direct

- Utilize simple sentence structures that are easy to follow

- Include subheaders and bullet points so that your letter is simple to read and your candidate can see what is on offer without working to find the information

- Make sure there is no ambiguity



## Can you use AI to make a job offer letter?

Yes. Generative AI tools like ChatGPT or Claude can help you draft a job offer letter faster. You’ll get better results if you give the tool an approved template and clear role details instead of asking it to start from scratch.

Start by downloading the template collection above. Choose the template that fits your offer, then use a prompt like this and fill in the brackets:

“You’re an HR Specialist at [Company name]. I’ve attached our approved offer letter template. Use it to draft a job offer letter for [Candidate name], who’s joining as [Job title] in [Department].

The start date is [Date]. The salary is [$amount] per [year/hour], paid [frequency]. Include these benefits: [list benefits]. The role is [on-site/remote/hybrid] and reports to [Manager name and title].

The candidate should respond by [Date]. The offer is contingent on [list contingencies, or write “no contingencies”].

Keep the template’s structure, at-will statement, and legal wording exactly as written. Use a warm, professional tone. Flag any missing details instead of guessing.”

Before you send the letter, check every name, date, salary figure, benefit, and contingency against your approved offer details. Ask your legal team to review any wording the tool changed, especially at-will language or contingencies.

You should also follow your company’s policy on using AI tools with candidate information. Avoid sharing personal or confidential data unless your organization has approved the tool for that purpose.

## How to send an offer letter

The way you share the offer shapes how the candidate feels about it. Here’s how to handle the moments before and after the letter goes out.

### Make a verbal offer first

Call the candidate to share the news before you send anything in writing. A verbal offer lets you hear their reaction, answer questions, and spot concerns about pay or start date early.

Keep the call brief. Confirm the job title, salary, and start date, and explain that the written offer will follow. Keep what you say on the call consistent with your at-will statement, covered in the legal language section above.

### Send the written offer letter and follow up

How you deliver the job offer is as generous as how it is written and what it contains. Most job offers are sent via email, but there may be a better method suited to the candidate in question; consider the candidate’s communication preferences. Send the written letter promptly after the verbal offer so the candidate has the full terms in writing. The job offer email template above makes this quick.

It’s also a good idea to follow up with the candidate, especially if you have included an offer deadline. If you haven’t heard back as the deadline approaches, send a short, friendly email to check in and offer a call.

Throughout, keep your tone warm and professional, since every message shapes your employer brand .

### Handle negotiations and counteroffers

You may find that your letter sparks negotiations or counteroffers. This is not a problem, as it may even be extremely positive for the long-term relationship you build with the candidate. It’s also far preferable to know what the candidate wants and why you may not have enticed them to accept an offer, so encourage transparency and open communication.

Prepare for the candidate’s response in advance, including how far you can move on salary, start date, or benefits. Be flexible within reason and in line with industry standards, and check any legal limits before you agree to new terms.

Ask the candidate to send you questions, listen to what they say, and treat the entire process as a two-way conversation rather than just sending an offer and expecting a yes or no reply.

### Confirm acceptance and start preboarding

Once the candidate signs, confirm that you’ve received the letter and share what happens next. Send an email with their first-day details, and start preparing their paperwork, equipment, and system access.

This period between acceptance and the first day is called preboarding , and it helps new hires feel ready before they start. Let the other candidates know the role has been filled, too.

## FAQ

What is the proper format for an offer letter? A standard offer letter opens with the date, the candidate’s details, and a personal greeting. It then covers the job title, start date, pay, benefits, work schedule, reporting line, and any contingencies. It closes with a response deadline, contact details, and signature lines.

How do you write an offer letter? Start with a personalized greeting and confirm the job title and start date. Then outline the salary, benefits, and schedule, add an at-will statement and any contingencies, and set a response deadline. Ask your legal team to review the letter before you send it.
