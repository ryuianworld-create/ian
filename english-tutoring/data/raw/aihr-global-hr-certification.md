## What is a global or international HR certification?

A global or international HR certification is aimed at HR professionals outside a specific location, such as the U.S. Each HR certification provider uses either term slightly differently, but typically, global HR certifications (e.g., HRCI’s GPHR certification) are aimed at HR professionals who have multinational responsibilities, like developing a global strategy.

International HR certifications, on the other hand, are aimed at HR professionals in international locations and cover broad HR knowledge or skills within a specific HR domain (like AIHR’s Certificate Programs , for example).

### 5. SHRM-CP (SHRM)

As one of the world’s largest HR associations, SHRM is recognized by multinational employers. The SHRM-CP , however, is built primarily around U.S. HR practice and as such, aimed mainly at U.S.-based HR professionals.

- Format and duration: 134 multiple-choice questions, four hours, in-person at a Prometric center.

- What you’ll learn: SHRM’s competency model, covering employee relations, talent, total rewards, and the behavioral competencies around them.

- Best for: Operational HR professionals, especially those working for U.S.-headquartered employers.

- Cost: $399 for members, $499 for non-members.

### 6. GPHR (HRCI)

The Global Professional in Human Resources (GPHR) is the only HRCI credential for HR that focuses on strategic global HR development, global talent management, and global mobility.

- Format and duration: Computer-based exam consisting of 100 questions, held at a Pearson VUE center or online via OnVUE over two hours 15 minutes. The credential is valid for three years, and renewal requires 45 HR and 15 global credits.

- What you’ll learn: Global strategy and development, global talent management, mobility, workplace culture, total rewards, and cross-border risk and compliance.

- Best for: HR leaders with multinational responsibility, and at least two years of professional HR experience with a master’s, three with a bachelor’s, or four with neither.

- Cost: $595 (made up of a $100 application fee and a $495 exam fee).

### 7. aPHRi (HRCI)

HRCI’s Associate Professional in Human Resources International (aPHRi) is designed for HR professionals working outside the U.S. The exam exclues U.S. employment law, and is also available in Arabic.

- Format and duration: Computer-based exam consisting of 65 questions, held over one hour and 45 minutes. The credential is valid for three years, and renewal requires 45 credits.

- What you’ll learn: HR operations, recruitment and selection, employee relations, compensation and benefits, health and safety, and development and retention.

- Best for: People entering HR outside the U.S., career changers, and managers without formal HR training.

- Cost: $400 (made up of a $100 application fee and $300 exam fee).

### 8. PHRi (HRCI)

The Professional in Human Resources International (PHRi), HRCI’s international practioner certification, is built for HR professionals running HR in markets outside the U.S., and is also offered in Spanish.

- Format and duration: Computer-based exam consisting of 90 questions, held over two hours 15 minutes. The credential is valid for three years, and renewal requires 60 credits.

- What you’ll learn: Operational HR applied in your local context, covering talent acquisition, employee relations, total rewards, learning and development, and HR information management outside the U.S.

- Best for: HR practitioners running day-to-day HR in a non-U.S. market. It requires one year of experience plus a master’s, two plus a bachelor’s, or four years with neither.

- Cost: $495 (made up of a $100 application fee and a $395 exam fee).

### 9. SPHRi (HRCI)

The Senior Professional in Human Resources International (SPHRi) is HRCI’s international senior-level certification. It’s meant for HR leaders outside the U.S. who set strategy, rather than run operations.

- Format and duration: Computer-based exam, held over two hours and 45 minutes at a Pearson VUE centre or online via OnVUE. The credential is valid for three years, and renewal requires 60 credits.

- What you’ll learn: Leadership and strategy, talent management, workforce planning, total rewards, HR information management, and safety and security, all at a strategic level.

- Best for: Senior HR professionals outside the U.S. Needs four years’ experience plus a master’s, five plus a bachelor’s, or seven years with neither, and HRCI expects documented knowledge of local employment law

- Cost: $595 (made up of a $100 application fee and a $495 exam fee).



### 10. CHRP (HRPA)

HRPA is Ontario’s HR regulatory body, so the Certified Human Resources Professional (CHRP) is a national rather than global designation. Canadian employers often expect it, and AIHR Certificate Programs earn you recertification credits toward it.

- Format and duration: Can be completed via part-time studies (12 to 22 months) or multi-part professional curricula (three to four years). The exam is held either as one-hour computer-based testing with open-book multiple-choice questions, or two-hour comprehensive subject challenge exams.

- What you’ll learn: Canadian employment law, HR practice, and professional ethics as applied in Ontario.

- Best for: HR professionals working in Canada, where the designation is often expected rather than optional.

- Cost: At least 695 CAD (350 CAD knowledge exam registration fee, 225 CAD employment law prep program, 120 to 141 CAD practice exams), plus an HRPA membership fee that varies by membership category.

### 11. Level 3 Foundation Certificate in People Practice (CIPD)

The U.K.-based CIPD’s qualifications are recognized across the U.K., Ireland, the Middle East, and parts of Asia. The Level 3 Foundation Certificate in People Practice is its entry-level HR certification, ideal for those new to HR or with minimal HR experience.

- Format and duration: Available 100% online (self-paced or live classes), part-time, or via evening classes. Typically takes eight to 12 months to complete.

- What you’ll learn: People practice fundamentals, covering business and culture in context, the principles of analytics, core behaviors for people professionals, and HR life cycle essentials.

- Best for: People new to HR, or those in an HR Administrator or HR Assistant role who want a recognized qualification without a postgraduate commitment. It leads to CIPD Foundation Membership.

- Cost: Between £1,300 and £2,300 (depending on the training provider and study format), plus CIPD membership (which varies by level).

### 12. Level 5 Associate Diploma in People Management (CIPD)

The Level 5 Associate Diploma in People Management is CIPD’s practitioner-level HR certification, and the most common entry point for HR professionals who already have experience behind them.

- Format and duration: Available 100% online (self-paced or structured), through live online classes, or part-time day/evening classes through approved training providers. Typically takes 12 to 18 months to complete.

- What you’ll learn: Organizational performance and culture in practice, evidence-based practice, professional behaviors, employment relationship management, talent management and workforce planning, and reward for performance and contribution.

- Best for: HR advisors, officers, and generalists in CIPD-recognized markets who want to move toward management, or who are building toward Level 7. It leads to CIPD Associate Membership.

- Cost: Between £1,600 and £3,600, (depending on the training provider and study format), plus CIPD membership (which varies by level).

### 13. Level 7 Advanced Diploma in Strategic People Management (CIPD)

The Level 7 Advanced Diploma in Strategic People Management is CIPD’s most senior qualification, recognized in the same markets its Level 3 and Level 5 diplomas are.

- Format and duration: Flexible online, hybrid, or classroom formats. Typically takes 16 to 24 months to complete (extendable up to 36 months for self-paced study).

- What you’ll learn: Strategic people management, organizational design and development, employment relations, and reward, at the postgraduate level.

- Best for: Senior HR professionals in the U.K., Ireland, the Middle East, and other CIPD-recognized markets. This is the level that leads toward Chartered Member status.

- Cost: Between £3,000 and £7,000, (depending on the training provider and study format), plus CIPD membership (which varies by level).

### 14. Global Remuneration Professional (WorldatWork)

The Global Remuneration Professional (GRP) is WorldatWork’s multi-country compensation and benefits certification, and is worth considering if your pay programs cross borders.

- Format and duration: Nine self-paced courses over one year, via flexible e-learning, virtual classes, or intensive regional fast-track pathways. Each course has its own exam (typically 80 questions each, with a two-hour time limit per exam).

- What you’ll learn: Designing and administering base and variable pay across multiple countries, job evaluation, pay structures, and the regional influences on reward design.

- Best for: Compensation and total rewards specialists whose programs span borders.

- Cost: $850 for members and $1,250 to $1,395 for non-members for each e-learning course and exam module.

### 15. Global Mobility Specialist (Worldwide ERC)

Worldwide ERC is the industry body for workforce mobility, and the Global Mobility Specialist (GMS) is the recognized credential for HR professionals responsible for moving employees between countries.

- Format and duration: 100% online via the Worldwide ERC Learning Portal, with comprehensive educational modules (including courses and quizzes). Typically requires approximately 25 hours of active study and coursework to complete, with a time limit of six months from first access.

- What you’ll learn: The mechanics of moving people between countries, including assignment structures, relocation policy, immigration and tax basics, and vendor management.

- Best for: HR and mobility professionals who own relocation or international assignment programs.

- Cost: $1,250 for members, $1,500 for non-members.

## How to get a global/international HR certification

Follow these six steps to learn how to get a global or international HR certification:

### Step 1: Decide on the type of HR certification

Start with your HR domain. If you have multinational responsibilities (e.g., developing global strategy across offices in different countries), a global certification like the GPHR is a good option. If you’re an HR professional based outside the U.S. who wants broad HR capabilities or depth in a specific domain like business partnering or people analytics , an international certification or a certificate program is a better fit.

### Step 2: Check the eligibility criteria

Requirements vary among providers, and most combine education with experience. HRCI’s aPHRi is knowledge-based and asks for no HR experience at all, while CIPD entry requirements are set by the approved center and rise with each level. AIHR’s Certificate Programs have no eligibility requirements, so you can start building capability while you accumulate the experience senior credentials ask for.

### Step 3: Prioritize preparation

While experience is crucial, adequate knowledge and preparation are key to acing the exam itself. Consider these resources to help you:

- Certificate programs: The Academy to Innovate HR (AIHR) offers Certificate Programs specifically designed to equip you with the knowledge you need to prepare for certain global HR certification exams. As an accredited HR program provider , you’ll also earn PDC credits when completing an AIHR Certificate Program.

- Self-study materials: Organizations that provide global HR certifications also tend to offer a wealth of self-study materials, including study guides, courses, and practice exams. Additionally, numerous online resources and textbooks are available to prepare you for exams.

### Step 4: Take the exam

Global HR certification exams are typically computer-based and consist of multiple-choice questions. Be sure to understand the exam format of your chosen certification program, its content focus areas, and exam time limits beforehand. Sourcing sample exam papers from legitimate online sites can be very helpful in this regard.

Scheduling and fees vary depending on the provider. Some organizations, such as HRCI, offer free ‘second chance insurance’, which is an opportunity to retake the exam at no cost if your initial results are unsatisfactory.

### Step 5: Recertification

Once you pass your global certification, it’s generally valid for a set period, after which it will lapse. For instance, the GPHR certification remains valid for three years after testing. To maintain your GPHR credentials, you must acquire 45 hours of HR training and 15 global recertification credits or retake the exam within the three-year period. AIHR is recognized by several major HR credentialing bodies, including HRCI, for recertification credits.

### Step 6: Ongoing development

The HR profession is constantly changing. Continuing to build on your education through certificate programs and courses can help you stay ahead in your profession.

Consider programs like AIHR’s Certificate Programs, which are focused on related emerging fields, such as the application of Artificial Intelligence for HR . These programs can also help you develop crucial digital skills to manage data, identify insights, track trends, and automate aspects of HR. These specializations can significantly enhance your professional acumen and open doors to exciting career opportunities.
