## What is an HR annual calendar?

An HR annual calendar is a 12-month plan of your organization’s key people activities and deadlines. It helps you see what needs to happen, when preparation should start, and how each activity connects to wider business priorities.

You may also hear it referred to as an HR calendar of activities or an HR planning calendar. Whatever the name, its purpose is the same: to help you coordinate HR work with the needs of the business and the people involved.

Most HR annual calendars include activities across four areas:

- Business and workforce planning: Hiring plans, HR budgets , and reviews of the skills and roles your organization needs.

- Talent and development: Goal setting, performance reviews , pay reviews, promotions, learning programs, and succession planning .

- Employee experience: Engagement surveys , wellbeing initiatives, recognition programs, and employee communications .

- Compliance and administration: Statutory reporting, policy reviews, payroll deadlines, mandatory training, and audits.

A useful calendar also shows how these activities fit together. For example, scheduling pay reviews after performance discussions gives managers time to use review outcomes in compensation decisions. Completing workforce planning before budget discussions also helps teams make clearer hiring and resourcing requests.

## Annual HR calendar of activities by quarter

Planning your HR priorities for 2027? This calendar gives you a place to start. Each quarter opens with a checklist to help you balance larger plans with day-to-day tasks, followed by key dates for each month.

Use the labels to quickly find holidays, compliance deadlines, and opportunities to connect with your team. U.S. federal holidays and filing deadlines are included, with dates adjusted where deadlines fall on weekends. Be sure to check any state, local, or industry requirements where your employees work, too.

### Q1 (January–March)

Q1 sets the tone for the whole year. It is when HR locks in priorities, closes out last year’s payroll and tax obligations, and gives managers what they need to start strong.

##### Q1 HR checklist

- Set your annual HR goals: Agree on three to five HR priorities with leadership and link each one to a business goal.

- Close out year-end payroll and tax filings: Confirm that all W-2s, 1099s, and quarterly filings are complete and accurate.

- Review and update your employee handbook: Reflect any new federal, state, or local laws that took effect at the start of the year.

- Kick off goal setting with managers: Share timelines and templates so individual goals are in place early in the year.

- Finalize your L&D plan and budget: Base it on skill gaps identified in last year’s performance reviews.

- Plan Q2 hiring: Confirm approved headcount with finance and open priority roles before spring.

#### January

January is about resetting priorities and wrapping up year-end reporting. Finalize HR goals with leadership early, so your team isn’t planning and executing at the same time.

- [Holiday] Jan. 1: New Year’s Day (federal holiday). Share updated policies and any benefit changes that take effect in the new year.

- [Compliance] Jan. 1: State and local minimum wage increases. Many states and cities raise minimum wages on this date. Confirm payroll rates and exempt salary thresholds for every work location.

- [Engagement] Jan. 1–31: National Mentoring Month. Launch or relaunch a mentoring program and match mentors with mentees.

- [Holiday] Jan. 18: Martin Luther King Jr. Day (federal holiday). Plan a day of service or a learning session on inclusion.

- [Engagement] Jan. 28: Data Privacy Day. Remind employees how you handle their personal data and refresh data protection training.

#### February

February wraps up year-end filings and shifts attention to the workforce itself. It is a useful time to finalize goals, prepare managers, and plan around cultural and religious observances.

- [Engagement] Feb. 1–28: Black History Month. Partner with employee resource groups on programming, and review representation data in hiring and promotion.

- [Compliance] Feb. 1: W-2, 1099-NEC, Form 940, and Q4 Form 941 deadline . The usual Jan. 31 deadline falls on a Sunday, so it moves to Monday. Double-check employee addresses in December to avoid returned forms.

- [Compliance] Feb. 1: OSHA Form 300A posting begins. Covered employers must post the annual injury and illness summary until Apr. 30.

- [Engagement] Feb. 6: Lunar New Year. Accommodate time-off requests and avoid scheduling critical deadlines around the celebration.

- [Engagement] Around Feb. 8: Ramadan begins (dates depend on moon sighting). Offer flexible scheduling, move team lunches and events where possible, and brief managers ahead of time.

- [Holiday] Feb. 15: Presidents’ Day (federal holiday). Many private employers stay open, so confirm that your holiday schedule has been communicated.

#### March

March closes the Q1 compliance cycle for ACA reporting and brings two of the year’s main recognition moments.

- [Engagement] Mar. 1–31: Women’s History Month. Review pay equity and promotion data by gender alongside any celebratory programming.

- [Compliance] Mar. 1: ACA Forms 1094-C and 1095-C paper filing deadline. Feb. 28 falls on a Sunday, so the deadline moves to Monday.

- [Compliance] Mar. 2: Form 1095-C furnishing deadline and OSHA 300A electronic submission. Employers may instead post a clear notice that employees can request their 1095-C. Establishments covered by OSHA’s electronic reporting rule must submit 300A data by this date.

- [Engagement] Mar. 5: Employee Appreciation Day. Coordinate recognition across teams so no group is overlooked.

- [Engagement] Mar. 8: International Women’s Day.

- [Compliance] Mar. 14: Daylight saving time begins. Check timekeeping for overnight shifts so non-exempt employees are paid for actual hours worked.

- [Compliance] Mar. 31: ACA electronic filing deadline for Forms 1094-C and 1095-C.

### Q2 (April–June)

Q2 is when the year’s plans start turning into day-to-day work. It is a good time to check hiring progress, prepare managers for mid-year performance conversations, and start reviewing benefits before renewal decisions pick up later in the year. Feedback quality matters in this quarter, too. Gallup data shows that 80% of employees who report receiving meaningful feedback in the past week are fully engaged.

##### Q2 HR checklist

- Prepare managers for mid-year reviews: Refresh templates, run short training on feedback conversations, and set a completion date in late June.

- Review hiring against the workforce plan: Compare filled roles to approved headcount, and track time to fill for critical positions.

- Analyze Q1 engagement and attrition data: Identify patterns by team or manager before mid-year reviews begin.

- Start the benefits review: Pull utilization and claims data, and gather employee feedback ahead of renewal discussions with brokers.

- Prepare EEO-1 data: Validate job categories and demographic data in your HRIS before the reporting window opens.

- Plan summer coverage: Confirm leave schedules and backfill arrangements for peak vacation months.

#### April

April is a good time to check employee wellbeing, safety, and reporting tasks before mid-year work intensifies.

- [Engagement] Apr. 1–30: Stress Awareness Month. Promote Employee Assistance Program (EAP) and mental health resources, and review workload data for teams under sustained pressure.

- [Engagement] Apr. 21: Administrative Professionals Day. Recognize the work of administrative professionals and review whether they have the tools, support, and development opportunities they need.

- [Engagement] Apr. 22: Earth Day and Take Our Daughters and Sons to Work Day. Both fall on the same day in 2027, so coordinate facilities, safety briefings, and communications.

- [Compliance] Apr. 28: World Day for Safety and Health at Work. Review incident trends and refresh safety training where needed.

- [Compliance] Apr. 30: Q1 Form 941 deadline. The OSHA 300A posting period also ends.

#### May

May brings several employee experience and reporting priorities. It is also a useful time to prepare for any wage changes that take effect mid-year.

- [Engagement] May 1–31: Mental Health Awareness Month. Remind employees of mental health coverage and EAP access, and equip managers to recognize signs of distress.

- [Engagement] May 1–31: Asian American, Native Hawaiian, and Pacific Islander Heritage Month. Partner with employee resource groups on programming, and review representation data in hiring, promotion, and development.

- [Compliance] Spring: EEO-1 Component 1 reporting. The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) sets collection dates each year, so confirm the portal opening and deadline for your organization.

- [Compliance] Late May: Check upcoming July 1 wage increases. Several states and cities adjust minimum wages mid-year.

- [Holiday] May 31: Memorial Day (federal holiday).

#### June

June is a natural point to finish mid-year preparation and review safety, inclusion, and summer coverage plans.

- [Engagement] Jun. 1–30: Pride Month. Pair visibility with a review of benefits and policies for LGBTQ+ employees and their families.

- [Compliance] Jun. 1–30: National Safety Month. Use this to complete any outstanding mandatory safety training before mid-year.

- [Holiday] Jun. 18: Juneteenth, observed (federal holiday). Jun. 19 falls on a Saturday, so the federal observance moves to Friday.

### Q3 (July–September)

Q3 is the planning quarter for the year ahead. Benefits renewals, compensation budgets, and Q4 hiring all need decisions before October. Cost pressure may make the benefits timeline tighter, so start early and give employees enough time to understand any plan changes. Mercer projects total health benefit cost per employee will rise 8.2% on average in the next year, even after planned cost-reduction measures.

##### Q3 HR checklist

- Finalize benefits renewals: Confirm rates and plan design with brokers and carriers, and model employee contribution changes.

- Prepare open enrollment communications: Draft materials, FAQs, and decision-support tools, and brief managers on how to answer questions.

- Open compensation planning with finance: Agree on merit, promotion, and bonus pool assumptions for the year-end cycle.

- Review H1 workforce data: Analyze engagement, performance, absence, and turnover trends to set Q4 priorities.

- Launch Q4 hiring: Open priority roles now to avoid competing with year-end slowdowns.

- Book year-end events: Secure venues, budgets, and dates for end-of-year recognition and celebrations.

#### July

July is a good time to check mid-year compliance updates, prepare for benefits deadlines, and review early Q4 hiring needs.

- [Compliance] Jul. 1: Mid-year minimum wage increases in several states and cities. Update payroll rates for affected locations.

- [Holiday] Jul. 5: Independence Day, observed (federal holiday). Jul. 4 falls on a Sunday, so the federal observance moves to Monday.

- [Engagement] Jul. 25: National Hire a Veteran Day. Review veteran recruitment channels and support programs.

- [Engagement] Jul. 29: National Intern Day. Recognize intern contributions and schedule conversion discussions for high performers.

#### August

August is a useful month to complete mid-year benefit filings, prepare for open enrollment, review safety programs, and check affordability rules for the next plan year.

- [Engagement] Aug. 1–31: National Wellness Month. Preview wellbeing benefits for the coming plan year.

- [Compliance] Aug. 2: Form 5500, Q2 Form 941, and PCORI fee deadline . Jul. 31 falls on a Saturday, so these move to Monday. Form 5500 applies to calendar-year plans without an extension.

- [Compliance] Around Aug. 9–15: OSHA Safe + Sound Week (dates to be confirmed by OSHA). Use the week to review safety and health programs, incident trends, and training gaps.

- [Compliance] Late summer: ACA affordability threshold for the next plan year. Check the IRS-published percentage and confirm that employee contributions for the lowest-cost plan remain affordable.

- [Engagement] Aug. 26: Women’s Equality Day.

#### September

September is when year-end planning starts to accelerate. Use it to recognize payroll work, prepare federal contractor reporting where applicable, and start Hispanic Heritage Month programming.

- [Holiday] Sep. 6: Labor Day (federal holiday).

- [Engagement] Sep. 6–10: National Payroll Week. Recognize the payroll team ahead of the heaviest part of the year.

- [Engagement] Sep. 10: World Suicide Prevention Day (September is Suicide Prevention Awareness Month). Recirculate crisis resources and Employee Assistance Program (EAP) details.

- [Engagement] Sep. 15–Oct. 15: Hispanic Heritage Month. Partner with employee resource groups on programming, and connect events to broader inclusion and career development work.

- [Compliance] Sep. 30: VETS-4212 deadline for covered federal contractors. This is also the general deadline for distributing the Summary Annual Report for calendar-year plans that filed Form 5500 without an extension.

### Q4 (October–December)

Q4 combines execution of the current year with planning for the next. Open enrollment, year-end reviews, compensation decisions, and the following year’s HR plan all compete for attention in a short period. As you build next year’s plan, include a clear position on HR technology and AI. Gartner advises HR leaders to develop an HR-specific AI strategy that aligns with broader business goals.

##### Q4 HR checklist

- Run open enrollment: Open the window, track completion rates weekly, and follow up with employees who haven’t made elections.

- Complete year-end reviews and calibration: Set calibration sessions before compensation decisions are locked.

- Finalize compensation decisions: Approve merit increases, promotions, and bonuses, and schedule effective dates with payroll.

- Build next year’s HR plan and budget: Align headcount, programs, and technology investment with finance and leadership.

- Prepare for Jan. 1 legal changes: Update policies, payroll settings, and the handbook for new laws taking effect.

- Verify year-end payroll data: Confirm employee addresses, tax withholding, and taxable benefits before W-2 production.

- Publish the holiday schedule: Confirm coverage plans for critical roles over the holiday period.

#### October

October is a busy month for benefits communication, inclusion activities, and early year-end preparation.

- [Engagement] Oct. 1–31: National Disability Employment Awareness Month. Review accommodation processes and the accessibility of internal systems and communications.

- [Engagement] Oct. 10: World Mental Health Day. It falls on a Sunday in 2027, so schedule activities for the surrounding week.

- [Holiday] Oct. 11: Columbus Day / Indigenous Peoples’ Day (federal holiday). Most private employers remain open, so confirm your policy.

- [Compliance] Oct. 14: Medicare Part D creditable coverage notices. Notices must reach Medicare-eligible participants before the Medicare annual enrollment period opens on Oct. 15.

- [Engagement] Oct. 15: Boss’s Day, observed. Boss’s Day falls on Saturday, Oct. 16, so many workplaces observe it on Friday.

#### November

November brings year-end reviews, payroll reporting, and career development activities. It is also a good time to check managers’ workload before the final planning push.

- [Compliance] Nov. 1: Q3 Form 941 deadline. Oct. 31 falls on a Sunday, so the deadline moves to Monday.

- [Engagement] Nov. 1–30: National Career Development Month. Align development planning with year-end review conversations.

- [Engagement] Nov. 3: International Stress Awareness Day. This coincides with peak review and planning workloads for many managers.

- [Compliance] Nov. 7: Daylight saving time ends. In most U.S. states, check overnight shift pay for the extra hour worked.

- [Holiday] Nov. 11: Veterans Day (federal holiday).

- [Holiday] Nov. 25: Thanksgiving Day (federal holiday). If your organization also observes Nov. 26, confirm which roles need coverage and communicate expectations early.

#### December

December is about closing out the year while preparing employees, payroll, and policies for January.

- [Compliance] Early December: Verify W-2 data. Confirm addresses, legal names, and Social Security numbers, and remind employees to update their records.

- [Engagement] Dec. 3: International Day of Persons with Disabilities.

- [Compliance] Mid-December: Flexible spending account reminders. Communicate use-it-or-lose-it deadlines, grace periods, or carryover limits.

- [Holiday] Dec. 24: Christmas Day, observed (federal holiday). Dec. 25 falls on a Saturday, so the federal observance moves to Friday.

- [Engagement] Dec. 24–Jan. 1: Hanukkah and Kwanzaa. Hanukkah begins at sundown on Dec. 24, and Kwanzaa runs from Dec. 26 to Jan. 1. Accommodate floating holiday requests and avoid assuming a single holiday calendar.

- [Holiday] Dec. 31: New Year’s Day, observed (federal holiday). Jan. 1, 2028, falls on a Saturday, so the federal observance moves to Friday.

## How to create an HR annual calendar

Use the quarterly calendar above as a starting point, then tailor it to your organization’s fiscal year, workforce footprint, compliance needs, and business peaks. Follow these six steps to build your own HR annual calendar.

### Map your business cycle

Start with your organization’s operating rhythm: fiscal year, budget deadlines, board meetings, peak trading periods, and planned system launches. These fixed points shape when HR activities can happen.

For example, if compensation budgets are due to finance in October, you may need performance data by September. Deloitte research found that 7 in 10 business leaders see speed and agility as their primary competitive strategy, so flag business dates that may shift.

### Add compliance deadlines first

Compliance obligations are date-specific and often non-negotiable, so add them first. Together, they form your HR compliance calendar. Include:

- Federal filings, such as payroll tax, W-2s, ACA reporting, Form 5500, and EEO-1

- State and local requirements, such as minimum wage changes, paid leave, and pay transparency

- Required postings

- Benefits notices.

Set internal due dates two to four weeks before each statutory deadline. For U.S. employers, the IRS employment tax calendar and EEOC EEO-1 updates are useful starting points, but multi-state employers should also track requirements by jurisdiction.

### List recurring HR activities by category

List every recurring people process, including performance reviews, merit cycles, open enrollment, surveys, talent reviews , and mandatory training.

For each activity, note how long it takes, who is involved, and what it depends on. This can help you spot activities that continue out of habit rather than clear business need.

### Assign owners and lead times

Give each activity one accountable owner and a preparation start date. Typical lead times include:

- Open enrollment: 90–120 days

- Compensation cycles: 8–12 weeks

- Engagement surveys: 6–8 weeks

- Performance reviews: 4–6 weeks

Build in manager capacity, too. Gartner’s research found that 47% of managers say more is expected of them than a year ago, and only 41% of employees say their manager helps them prioritize their work.

### Flag dependencies and conflicts

Map how activities depend on each other. Performance ratings may feed merit decisions, talent reviews may inform succession plans, and engagement results may shape action plans.

Before publishing the calendar, check for activities that run in the wrong order, overlap with business peaks, or create too much pressure for the same teams.

### Share it and review it quarterly

Publish the calendar where managers already work, such as a shared calendar, HRIS , or planning tool. Walk managers through the periods that need their input, clarify ownership, and review the calendar each quarter against business conditions. Note what ran late, what clashed, and what no longer adds enough value, then rebuild the calendar annually alongside budget planning.
