## Do you need a certification to become an HR Manager?

No, but it can help you get there faster. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) notes that HR managers typically need a bachelor’s degree and several years of related work experience, and certification can help your résumé stand out to prospective employers.

The role pays well and continues to grow. BLS reported a median annual wage of $140,030 for HR managers in May 2024, and projects 5% employment growth to 2034. When several experienced candidates compete for the same role, those with a recognized HR Manager certification is likely to have an edge over their competitors.

Hiring managers still want to see what you’ve done (e.g., a policy you rewrote, or managers you coached), and the right certification can strengthen these achievements. If you’re not sure you need a management-level credential yet, check out AIHR’s guides to Human Resources management certifications and how to become HR certified to learn about broader options.

It’s also worth noting the difference between a certification and a certificate program. A certification is an exam-based credential awarded by a certifying body, such as SHRM or HRCI. You meet eligibility criteria, pass a proctored exam, and keep the credential active by earning recertification credits. Essentially, it confirms that your knowledge meets industry standards.

A certificate program, such as those AIHR offers , is a structured course of study that ends with a certificate of completion or achievement. Strong programs include projects, templates, and assessments you complete along the way, and help you build skills you can apply at work right away.

## What skills should an HR Manager course build?

When you move into management, the work shifts from running HR processes to shaping how HR supports the business. A strong program should prepare you for that shift in these five areas:

- Business and strategic alignment: Connecting HR priorities to business goals, and explaining your plans in terms leaders care about, such as cost, risk, and growth.

- HR operating models: Understanding how HR is structured and how services reach employees, so you can decide where business partners, centers of expertise, and shared services fit.

- Change and culture leadership: Leading change programs , building buy-in, and handling resistance without losing trust.

- Stakeholder management: Mapping who influences a decision, planning how you’ll communicate with them, and clarifying who owns each task on a project.

- Team leadership: Delegating, HR team development , and remote or global HR team management .

Use these areas as a checklist when you review a syllabus. Exam-based credentials tend to test your knowledge of them, while applied programs should give you a chance to practice them through projects you can reuse.

Examples include a stakeholder map, an HR strategy draft, or a proposed operating model. Those work samples are also useful in interviews, because they show how you think as a manager.

## How to choose the right option for your career stage

The right choice depends on your current career stage and whether a certification or certificate program can help prepare you for an HR Manager role at that stage. Find the situation closest to yours below:

### You’re aiming for your first HR Manager role

Start by building management-level skills, especially if you don’t yet meet the experience requirements for the PHR or SHRM-SCP. A skills-based program such as AIHR’s HR Manager Certificate Program helps you practice strategy, operating models, and stakeholder work before you’re accountable for them.

If you want a formal credential as well, the SHRM-CP doesn’t require any experience, and there are also routes to HR certification without a degree .

### You’ve just stepped into management

Your first months will test how you prioritize, lead your team, and work with senior leaders. Applied learning usually pays off faster than exam preparation at this stage, because you can use it immediately.

Look for a program that helps you with work you already do (e.g., a stakeholder map for your first leadership meetings, or a RACI template that clarifies who owns each HR process). The HR Manager Certificate Program includes guides and templates for all three, and can help you show how you’ve shaped your company’s HR function in a performance review.

### You’re moving into a strategic or senior role

Once you meet the experience criteria, the SHRM-SCP or SPHR can validate strategic-level expertise against a recognized standard. Check your eligibility against the work you’ve done.

SHRM counts work such as developing HR policies, overseeing integrated HR operations , and aligning HR strategy with organizational goals, and you need at least 1,000 hours of it per calendar year. HRCI, on the other hand, designed the SPHR for professionals who plan HR policy rather than implement it.

### You already hold a SHRM or HRCI credential

You need recertification credits every three years: 60 PDCs for SHRM, and 60 credits for the PHR and SPHR. The SPHR also requires at least 15 of those to be Business credits. A mix of webinars can get you to 60, but it likely doesn’t teach you new HR skills. A program that builds new skills and earns credits means your learning hours, however, count toward both goals.

AIHR’s HR Manager Certificate Program , for example, is eligible for 22 SHRM PDCs (more than a third of your SHRM requirement), and 22 HRCI Business credits (more than the SPHR’s Business minimum). Credit amounts vary by program, so check the provider’s listing before you enroll.

### You work outside the U.S.

PHR and SPHR content is grounded in U.S. laws and regulations, so check what employers in your market recognize. HRCI offers international versions, and CIPD is widely recognized in the U.K. AIHR’s Certificate Programs are suited for any HR professional, regardless of your location. The programs are available online and are self-paced. AIHR is also globally recognized by 15+ HR associations, including SHRM, HRCI, HRPA, CPHR, ATD, and CIPD for recertification.
