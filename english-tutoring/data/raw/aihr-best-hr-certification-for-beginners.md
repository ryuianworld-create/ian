## How to choose the best HR certification for beginners

The best HR certification for beginners is one you can start now, without eligibility requirements, and that helps you build the skills you need in your first role. Recognition, cost, and study time matter too, but they’re easier to weigh once you know what you need a program to do for you.

Before you compare HR certifications, get clear on what you need the certification to do for you. A student with no work experience, a career changer, and a new HR Coordinator may all be beginners, but they don’t need the same learning path. Start with these five questions.

- What is my next career step? Are you applying for your first HR role, moving into HR from another function, or building confidence in a new HR job?

- Do I need broad HR knowledge or a specialist path? If you’re still exploring HR, choose a certification that covers the full employee lifecycle. If you already know you want to work in recruitment, learning and development, people analytics, or employee relations, a more focused program may be a better fit.

- What will I need to do on the job ? If you’re moving into an HR role, review the job description. Then check whether the program you’re considering lets you practice those tasks, or only tests what you know about them.

- How much structure do I need? Some beginners prefer a guided course with practical assignments, while others are ready to study independently for an exam. If you’ve never done HR work, assignments and roleplays give you a safe place to practice before you’re doing it for real.

- What proof do I want at the end? A certificate of completion, exam-based credential, or portfolio project can all help, but they serve different goals.

Once you know what you need, it becomes easier to compare your options. Use the five criteria below to separate a strong first HR certification from one that may not fit your goals.

### 6 criteria for choosing your first HR certification

- Job readiness: When you finish, will you be able to do the work, such as drafting a policy, running onboarding, or handling a difficult conversation, or will you mainly have proven what you know?

- Eligibility: Can you start now, or do you need experience before you’re allowed to enroll or sit the exam?

- Learning format: Is it a high-stakes exam, a coursework-based qualification, or a self-paced online program with a capstone project? If you’re new to HR, look for practical assignments that let you practice before you’re doing the work for real.

- Time: How long will it take to go from “I’m starting” to “I’m certified”? Check whether you can start right away or need to book a fixed testing window.

- Cost: What is the all-in price, including the application fee, exam fee, prep materials, and recertification? Check whether the price includes training or only the exam.

- Recognition: Does the credential carry weight with employers in the country and industry you want to work in? Check, too, whether the learning earns recertification credits toward a credential you might add later.



## The best HR certifications for beginners, compared

Beginners usually choose between two kinds of programs: ones that teach you the practical skills to do the job, and ones that test what you know. Skills-based certificate programs, like AIHR’s HR Generalist and HR Coordinator Certificate Programs, have no eligibility requirements and build the skills you’ll use in your first role. Exam-based credentials like the aPHR and SHRM-CP show employers what you know. Because the fees for exams like the aPHR and SHRM-CP cover your application and the exam itself, you’ll usually find and pay for your own study materials on top.

When you start your first HR job, you’re expected to answer policy questions, support onboarding, and handle sensitive conversations from the first week, and those are skills you build by practicing them. That’s why many people start with practical skills and add a credential later, once they have some experience to anchor what the exam tests. We’ve listed the skills-based options first for that reason.

### aPHR (HRCI)

The aPHR is built for people who are new to HR and have no HR experience. For students about to graduate or career changers building their first HR résumé, the Associate Professional in Human Resources (aPHR) from the HR Certification Institute (HRCI) is a common first credential.

It’s a knowledge-based credential, so it shows employers you understand HR fundamentals, but the fees cover the application and exam rather than training. You’ll prepare on your own or with a separate prep course.

- Best for : True beginners with no HR experience, including students, recent graduates, and career changers

- Experience required : No prior HR work experience

- What it covers : Entry-level HR fundamentals and basic HR knowledge. It signals commitment to learning the foundations of HR, but doesn’t replace hands-on experience.

- Format: Computer-based exam, in person at a Pearson VUE center, or online via OnVUE with 65 scored questions and 25 unscored pretest questions in 1 hour and 45 minutes.

- Cost :

- $100 non-refundable application fee

- $300 exam fee, which is nonrefundable once your application is approved

- $400 in total, plus any study materials you choose

- Credential validity : Three years

- Recertification : 45 recertification credits every three years, or retake the exam. AIHR’s certificate programs are eligible for HRCI recertification credits.

- Keep in mind: The aPHR tests what you know rather than how you apply it, so if you want to practice tasks like onboarding or policy writing before your first role, you’ll need to build those skills separately.

### SHRM-CP (SHRM)

The SHRM-CP suits U.S. beginners who already have some HR exposure, such as an HR-adjacent role, an internship, or HR coursework. SHRM-CP initially required one to four years’ HR experience (depending on your education), but the Society for Human Resource Management (SHRM) has removed this requirement. However, the exam is still for those who can apply HR judgment in workplace scenarios. It has 134 questions and takes about four hours, including 3 hours and 40 minutes of testing time, and about 40% of its questions are situational judgment items.

- Best for : People performing general HR or HR-related duties, currently enrolled HR students, and people pursuing a career in Human Resource Management (HRM)

- Experience required: No degree or specific HR experience. SHRM recommends a basic working knowledge of HR practices.

- What it covers: HR knowledge and behavioral competencies, tested through knowledge-based and situational judgment questions that assess workplace decision-making.

- Format: In-person exam at a Prometric testing center, taken during fixed testing windows (currently two a year), with application deadlines several months ahead

- Cost:

- Early bird rate of $350 for SHRM members and $450 for nonmembers, including a $50 nonrefundable application fee

- Standard rate of $399 for members and $499 for nonmembers

- Student rate of $150 to $299, depending on membership and timing

- Credential validity: Three years

- Recertification : 60 Professional Development Credits (PDCs) every three years, plus a processing fee of $165 for members or $210 for nonmembers. You can earn PDCs through AIHR’s certificate programs.

- Keep in mind: Because so much of the exam tests workplace judgment, beginners with no HR experience may find it easier to prepare once they’ve practiced real HR tasks. You’ll also need to plan your exam date around the testing windows, rather than starting whenever you’re ready.

### CIPD Level 3 Foundation Certificate in People Practice

The CIPD Level 3 Foundation Certificate in People Practice is designed for beginners in the U.K. and other CIPD-led markets. Explore the Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development (CIPD) if you want to work in HR in the U.K., Ireland, the Middle East, parts of Asia, or other regions that follow U.K. qualification standards. CIPD offers the Level 3 Foundation Certificate in People Practice for people starting their HR careers.

- Best for: Beginners in the U.K., as well as international markets that widely recognize CIPD.

- Experience requirements: None. CIPD describes it as suited to people who are new to the profession or working in an HR support role.

- What it covers: Foundational HR and people practice knowledge through coursework, across four units: business, culture and change in context; principles of analytics; core behaviors for people professionals; and essentials of people practice. It’s a qualification-based route rather than a one-day exam.

- Course/exam format: Delivered online, face to face, or blended by more than 170 approved centers worldwide, and typically completed part-time over eight to 12 months. Learners are assessed through coursework, instead of a single exam.

- Cost breakdown: Typically £1,300 to £2,300 in the U.K., depending on the center and study mode. CIPD student membership is included while you study.

- Credential validity: Not a time-limited credential; completion leads to CIPD Foundation Membership.

- Recertification requirements: None. CIPD members can record AIHR programs as verified continuing professional development (CPD).

- Keep in mind: At eight to 12 months, it’s the longest commitment on this list, and its recognition is only strongest in the U.K. and other markets that follow CIPD standards.

## Which HR certification should you get first? A simple decision framework

Answer three questions in order. Your experience narrows the field, your goal decides between skills and a credential, and your available time settles the choice.

- What is your work situation?

- If you’re about to start, or have just started, your first HR role, begin with the skills you’ll use every day. AIHR’s HR Coordinator Certificate Program fits HR Assistant, HR Administrator, and HR Coordinator roles, while the HR Generalist Certificate Program fits first HR hires and HR teams of one.

- If you have no HR experience and a budget under $500, the aPHR is a good option. It requires no experience and has a lower upfront cost than most other professional certifications, although you’ll need to prepare on your own.

- If you’re exploring HR as a career and want to learn the fundamentals first, consider HRCI’s Human Resource Associate Professional Certificate on Coursera. It’s a flexible, subscription-based option for complete beginners.

- If you’re preparing for your first HR Assistant, HR Administrator, or HR Coordinator role, AIHR’s HR Coordinator Certificate Program helps you develop the practical skills needed for everyday HR operations.

- If you already have some HR experience, consider the SHRM-CP to validate your knowledge. The PHR is another option once you meet its education and experience requirements.

- If you’re starting an HR career in the U.K. or another CIPD-recognized market, CIPD Level 3 is a good entry-level qualification.

- Do you need to build the skills for your first role, or prove what you already know?

- If you want a recognized professional credential for your résumé, consider an exam-based certification like the aPHR or SHRM-CP. Both demonstrate your HR knowledge to employers.

- If you want both, start with skills and add the credential once you have some experience. When you hold a SHRM or HRCI credential, AIHR’s certificate programs can also count toward your recertification credits.

- If you want to develop practical HR skills, AIHR’s HR Coordinator Certificate Program focuses on HR operations, while its HR Generalist Certificate Program covers a broader range of HR responsibilities.

- If you want to build foundational HR knowledge before pursuing a professional certification, HRCI’s Coursera program offers structured training and a certificate of completion.

- How much time can you commit?

- If you want to develop practical HR skills at your own pace, consider AIHR’s HR Coordinator or HR Generalist Certificate Programs. Both are fully online and self-paced, so you can fit your learning around your schedule with a suggested pace of three to four hours a week over 12 weeks, and you can start the day you enroll rather than waiting for a testing window.

- If you can dedicate one to three months to exam preparation, the aPHR is a good starting point. It’s one of the faster entry-level certifications to prepare for.

- If you have several months available, consider the SHRM-CP, which typically requires more extensive preparation, or HRCI’s Coursera program, which takes approximately five months at six hours per week.

- If you’re willing to commit up to a year, CIPD Level 3 typically takes eight to 12 months and involves coursework and written assessments.
